Trust layer for autonomous systems

Know when your systems are confidently wrong.

An independent trust layer for autonomous systems, integrated with allied sensing, ISR and edge-AI platforms.

How it works Work with us

The problem

Sensors fail quietly.

Satellite navigation drops under ice, inside mines and beside steel. Inertial sensors drift. Radios degrade. Most autonomous systems keep going on whatever data they have left.

Nothing tells the operator how much to believe it. The failure is not the loss of a sensor. It is not knowing that it has been lost.

What we are building

A second opinion for every autonomous system.

Ananta Meridian is developing an independent trust layer. It listens to the sensor data a system already produces, compares the sources against one another, and reports how far to trust the result, with the reasons.

It does not steer, control or replace the platform. It adds one thing the platform usually lacks: an honest account of its own confidence.

HIGH

Independent sources agree. The result can be relied on.

MEDIUM

Some sources are degraded or disagree. Use with care and read the reasons.

LOW

Sources conflict or are missing. Do not rely on this result without another check.

TRUST: MEDIUM
Reason: satellite position disagrees with inertial estimate
Reason: acoustic source unavailable
Suggested action: confirm position by another means

Where it sits

Sense. Understand. Decide. Act.

Sense

Sensors and platforms collect the raw data. Ours or allied; the trust layer does not care.

Understand

The trust layer compares sources and measures how far they can be believed.

Decide

Operators and planners see the result with its trust level and reasons.

Act

The platform acts. Control stays with the system and the people responsible for it.

Where we are

Early stage, and plain about it.

Our approach has been tested offline, on recorded and simulated data. It has not yet been field-tested.

What comes next is a short series of small, honest evidence steps, starting with our own data and a controlled loss of satellite navigation. We publish the sequence, and label what is planned as planned.
